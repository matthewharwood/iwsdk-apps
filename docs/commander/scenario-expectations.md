# Independent Commander scenario expectations

These expected results were derived from the official rules text, without reading the new engine's implementation or using its outputs as an oracle. They are specifications for executable tests, **not claims that those tests have run or passed**. Small constructed board states below are rule-unit fixtures; they do not count as legal 100-card full games or establish the complete source-pool release gate.

## Pinned source and conventions

The [official rules page](https://magic.wizards.com/en/rules) linked [MagicCompRules 20260819.txt](https://media.wizards.com/2026/downloads/MagicCompRules%2020260819.txt) when retrieved on 2026-09-06. The downloaded bytes have SHA-256 `4381ad1b39ab2c05f7d03633a20f711ed37277074d3266dcba5f38cbb527423f`. The document's own effective date is **August 7, 2026**; August 19 in the filename is not substituted for that date. Rule numbers below refer to those exact bytes. The acquisition worker independently confirmed identical bytes at `.commander/sources/archives/4381ad1b39ab2c05f7d03633a20f711ed37277074d3266dcba5f38cbb527423f.txt` (977,822 bytes).

Unless a row says otherwise: use ordinary Commander, no variant overrides, no teams, no limited range of influence, turn order A→B→C→D→A (or A→B→A), and A is active. Players start at 40 life. All objects have only the explicitly stated relevant characteristics/abilities; there are no prevention, replacement, continuous or triggered effects that alter the result. A plain creature is a controlled nonbattle creature with no abilities. Creature controller/owner are the same unless stated. Unit-test state construction must identify that it bypasses full deck setup. Full-game tests must independently validate deck legality, shuffled setup and supported programs.

The application may hide automatic transitions between stable decisions, but it must preserve the order and consequences below. It may not skip meaningful priority, choices, state-based actions (SBAs), triggers or chance events. An unimplemented row should have an explicit unsupported status, rather than a guessed expected result copied from the implementation.

## Setup and ordinary two/four-seat differences

| ID | Setup/action | Independent expected result and rule basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SET-01 | Each player supplies a valid 100-card deck with one commander; move through initial setup without mulligans. | Commander is face up in command zone, not shuffled into library. Each player has 40 life, 7 cards in hand, 92 in library and 1 commander. This count assumes one commander and no other pregame effects. CR 103.2c, 103.3, 903.5a, 903.6–7. |
| SET-02 | Compare the first turn in a game that began with 2 seats against one that began with 4. | A skips their first draw step in the 2-seat game: absent other effects, hand remains 7. A takes their first draw in the 4-seat game: hand 8/library 91. B takes their first draw in both. This is not Duel Commander or Brawl. CR 100.1a–b, 103.8a–c. |
| SET-03 | A takes one mulligan then keeps; compare 2 seats with 4. | 2 seats: draw 7, choose 1 to bottom, keep 6/library 93. 4 seats: first mulligan is free, keep 7/library 92. A second mulligan then keep yields 5/library 94 in 2 seats and 6/library 93 in 4. Bottom order is a player choice; the identity and order must not be auto-selected by the rules core. CR 103.5, 103.5c. |
| SET-04 | A keeps; B mulligans; in 4 seats C also mulligans and D keeps. | Collect each round's declarations in turn order, then perform that round's mulligans together. A and D cannot rejoin later rounds. B/C continue until they keep or reach the allowed zero-card boundary. Do not accidentally expose another player's private new hand in the projection. CR 103.5, 103.5c; hand visibility CR 402.3. |
| SET-05 | Validate a 100-card deck against its designated commander and snapshot legality. | Reject 99/101 total cards; reject duplicate ordinary nonbasic English names; allow repeated basic lands. Require every included card's identity within the commander's identity and the separate basic-land-type production restriction. Card-text exceptions and alternate commander arrangements require explicit supported rules, not a generic bypass. CR 903.4–5, 101.1. |
| SET-06 | Exercise commander-role classification separately from main-deck eligibility. | The pinned 903.3 allows a legendary creature card, a legendary Vehicle card, or a legendary Spacecraft card with one or more P/T boxes. Explicit card abilities can also modify eligibility under 903.3a. Do not substitute the older creature-only rule, accept every legendary card, or treat a main-deck legality flag as commander-role eligibility. |
| SET-07 | A 4-seat game later has only A/B left. | It is still a game that began as multiplayer; removing seats does not recreate a new 2-seat game or rerun setup. Keep original format configuration, turn history and starting-player metadata. CR 100.1a–b, 800.4. |

## Priority, stack and automatic boundaries

| ID | Setup/action | Independent expected result and rule basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PRI-01 | A has priority with empty stack during a main phase; each seat passes once. | 2 seats require A then B; 4 require A, B, C, D. Only after the complete consecutive pass cycle does the phase end. A pass by the wrong seat is not legal. CR 117.3d, 117.4. |
| PRI-02 | A casts a legal spell S. | S is on stack with its costs paid and cast triggers processed before priority. A receives priority again, not B. A may act again or pass; casting alone does not resolve S. CR 601.2i, 117.3c, 117.5. |
| PRI-03 | A's S is on stack; A passes, B casts R. | Stack top is R above S; B gets priority again. In 4 seats, B, C, D, A passing resolves R only. Then A, the active player, gets priority with S still on stack. A fresh pass cycle is required for S. CR 117.3b–d, 117.4, 117.7. |
| PRI-04 | A/B passed in a 4-seat stack window; C takes a legal action, including an immediate mana activation. | Earlier passes no longer form a consecutive all-pass cycle. C receives priority after their action; a fresh C, D, A, B pass cycle is needed. Do not resolve after merely accumulating 4 historical pass actions. CR 117.3c–d, 117.4. |
| PRI-05 | S resolves and a creature has lethal marked damage; one ordinary triggered ability per remaining player awaits stacking. | Perform simultaneous applicable SBAs and repeat to stability. Put ordinary triggers on stack in APNAP order, each controller choosing their own order; repeat checks as needed before giving the appropriate seat priority. With one ordinary trigger each and A active, D's is top in 4 seats. CR 117.5, 603.3b, 704.3. |
| PRI-06 | Untap begins, or cleanup has no exceptional SBA/trigger event. | No priority in untap. Normal cleanup performs required discard, clears marked damage/ends appropriate effects, and ends without a priority choice. If cleanup has relevant SBAs/triggers, process them and provide priority, then begin another cleanup after the stack empties/all pass. CR 117.3a, 514.1–3. |
| PRI-07 | A floats mana and passes; then the phase/step ends. | Passing does not empty A's mana. End of the step/phase empties unspent mana unless an explicit effect says otherwise. Do not drain mana on each command or carry it to another phase by default. CR 106.4, 106.4b. |
| PRI-08 | A resolving spell asks B to choose something. | B owns that choice despite A being active. No player has priority during that resolution choice; an ordinary cast command is not available. After completion, process SBAs/triggers before A's priority. CR 117.2e, 117.3b, 117.5. |

CR 603.3b in this source uses two APNAP passes: ordinary-event triggers first, then triggers whose condition is another ability triggering. PRI-05 deliberately uses only the first category. A single blanket trigger sort is not complete support for that rule.

## Casting, lands and payment

| ID | Setup/action | Independent expected result and rule basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CAST-01 | A has priority during their main phase with empty stack and an unused land play; play a basic land from hand. | Move it directly to battlefield; no stack object or response to the land play itself. A keeps priority. Reject a second land play without an additional-play effect, a land play on another player's turn, or a land play with a nonempty stack under this ordinary permission. CR 305.1–3, 117.3c. |
| CAST-02 | A plain creature spell costs {2}{G}. Available mana: G, U, U. | The colored requirement consumes G and generic 2 may consume U, U. Mana value 3 alone is not enough: U, U, U cannot pay it. Costs must be payable in full; an invalid payment does not partially spend mana or successfully cast the card. CR 107.4a–b, 601.2f–h. |
| CAST-03 | Cast the same ordinary creature spell in A's main/empty-stack window, then attempt it on B's turn or while another spell is pending. | First timing is permitted with priority/payment. Other timings are rejected absent flash or an explicit permission. An instant has the ordinary priority-based timing. CR 117.1a, 601.3, 702.8. |
| CAST-04 | Activate a basic Forest's intrinsic tap mana ability during priority or the casting mana-payment window. | Forest taps and adds G immediately; no respondable mana stack object. A tapped Forest cannot pay its tap cost again. An ordinary noncreature land is not stopped by creature summoning sickness. CR 305.6, 605.1a, 605.3a–c, 302.6. |
| CAST-05 | Announce modes, targets and division for a spell that needs them, then reach payment. | Choices and their constraints precede locked cost/payment. The same target cannot occupy two slots of one instance of the word target, but distinct target clauses may permit it. A required division gives each target at least 1. No opponent receives priority between declaration and payment. CR 601.2b–i, 117.1. |
| CAST-06 | S targets one creature; while S waits, that creature leaves its zone and later returns. | The returned permanent is a new object, not the old legal target. If all targets are now illegal, S does not resolve and its spell card goes to owner's graveyard. If another target remains legal, follow 608.2b's partial-resolution rules. Do not silently retarget to the new incarnation. CR 400.7, 608.2b. |
| CAST-07 | A cost reduction applies when total cost is determined, but paying the spell's sacrifice cost removes its source. | Determine and lock the cost first; paying the sacrifice does not retroactively increase that cast's locked cost. An unimplemented cost modifier must not be ignored. CR 601.2f–h. |

Current mana-ability classification also requires that the ability's cost and effect move no card to or from a library (CR 605.1a). Therefore a broader predicate based only on producing mana/no target/no loyalty is incorrect for the pinned release. CAST-04 does not establish support for arbitrary mana abilities.

## Commander casting and zone transitions

| ID | Setup/action | Independent expected result and rule basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CMD-01 | A owns commander K costing {2}{G}; cast K from command zone repeatedly with no modifiers. | The first costs {2}{G}, the second {4}{G}, the third {6}{G}. Track previous casts from command zone for that caster and that commander card. A successfully cast but later countered K still increments the history; an illegal/rejected proposal does not. CR 903.8, 601.2i. |
| CMD-02 | After K was cast twice from command zone, return it to hand and cast from hand. | That hand cast has no commander tax and does not increment the command-zone cast count. A later command-zone cast still uses the previous command-zone count. Zone changes do not erase the commander's designation or historical cast count. CR 903.3, 903.8. |
| CMD-03 | K moves from battlefield to graveyard and survives there until the next SBA check. | It entered graveyard, so relevant dies events can exist. At the SBA check its owner chooses whether to move it to command zone. This is not a blanket replacement that prevents entering graveyard. After the choice, repeat SBAs and process waiting triggers before priority. CR 903.9a, 704.6d, 117.5. |
| CMD-04 | K enters exile; owner declines the first available command-zone SBA choice. | K remains exiled. Later unrelated priority windows do not offer the same move again merely because K is a commander in exile; 903.9a requires arrival since the previous SBA check. A qualifying new zone entry can create a new choice. |
| CMD-05 | K would move to its owner's hand/library; owner accepts command zone instead. | Replace that move before hand/library entry. If owner declines, make the original move and do not offer the graveyard/exile SBA rule in hand/library. The owner makes the decision even if another seat controls K or the moving effect. CR 903.9b. |
| CMD-06 | B has 20 recorded combat damage from commander K and plenty of life; K deals B 1 more combat damage. | B loses at the next SBA check, regardless of life total. Combat damage from another commander is separate; noncombat damage from K does not raise this tally. Recasting or changing controller of K does not reset that card's damage history. CR 903.3, 903.10a, 704.6c. |

## Combat and damage decisions

| ID | Setup/action | Independent expected result and rule basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| COM-01 | A chooses attackers among: an untapped plain creature controlled since before the turn, a tapped one, a newly controlled one, and a newly controlled hasty one. | The first and hasty one may attack; tapped/newly controlled nonhasty ones may not. A creature with defender may not attack absent an override. Attacking normally taps a chosen creature; vigilance prevents that tap. It does not let a creature already tapped be declared as an attacker. CR 302.6, 508.1a/c/f, 702.3, 702.10, 702.20. |
| COM-02 | A declares attacks and the active-player priority window follows. | Attack declaration is a turn-based action, not a stack item. Finish the full declaration, process attack triggers, then give A priority before blockers are declared. All pass on an empty stack advances to blockers; do not combine declaration and blocking into one uninterruptible combat step. CR 508.1–2, 117.4–5. |
| COM-03 | In 4 seats A's X attacks B and Y attacks C. B has one untapped blocker; C has one. | B declares their blocks first, then C in APNAP order. Each can block only with their own creatures and only attacks directed at them/their controlled planeswalkers/protected battles. B cannot assign a blocker to Y. Finish declarations and triggers, then A gets priority. CR 802.3–4, 509.1–2. |
| COM-04 | A 2/2 flying attacker attacks B; B has a plain 2/2 and a 2/2 with reach. | The plain ground creature cannot block that flier; the reach creature can. A flying blocker may block a ground attacker. Summoning sickness does not stop an otherwise legal untapped creature from blocking. CR 509.1a, 702.9b, 702.17b, 302.6. |
| COM-05 | B declares one plain blocker for an attacker, then that blocker is removed before damage. | The attacker remains blocked. Without trample it assigns no damage if no blockers remain. With trample it may assign all its combat damage to the attacked player under 702.19d. Do not recompute blocked status from current blocker-list length. CR 509.1h, 510.1c, 702.19d. |
| COM-06 | A's plain 3/3 is blocked by B's plain 3/3; no further actions. | Each assigns 3 to the other; both deal damage simultaneously. Both are destroyed in the following SBA event. No first death prevents the other's normal simultaneous damage; no player acts between assignment and damage. CR 510.1–3, 704.5g. |
| COM-07 | A's plain 5/5 is blocked by two plain 3/3s; A chooses a 2/3 damage division. | This division is legal. The blocker assigned 3 is destroyed, the blocker assigned 2 survives with 2 marked, and attacker is destroyed by 6 simultaneous damage. The pinned rules do not impose the older damage-assignment ordering/lethal-to-first prerequisite for a nontrampling attacker. A controller must be able to choose among legal distributions. CR 510.1c–e, 510.2, 704.5g. |
| COM-08 | A's 5/5 trample is blocked by B's 2/2 with no marked damage. | Legal chosen assignment 2 to blocker / 3 to B deals those values; blocker dies and attacker survives with 2 marked. Assignment 1 to blocker / 4 to B is illegal. Assignment 5 to blocker / 0 to B is legal: trample does not force maximum player damage. CR 702.19a–b, 510.2. |
| COM-09 | A's 5/5 trample attacks through one 2/2 already marked with 1 damage, still alive. | Assigning 1 to blocker and 4 to B is legal. Trample lethal calculation accounts for already marked damage. Prevention/indestructibility do not lower the lethal amount assigned simply because actual destruction/damage could differ. CR 702.19b. |
| COM-10 | A's 5/5 deathtouch+trample is blocked by B's 4/4. | Chosen assignment 1 to blocker / 4 to B is legal; deathtouch makes nonzero assigned combat damage lethal for excess calculation. After damage the 4/4 is destroyed at SBAs; attacker survives 4 marked. Without deathtouch, the 4/4 needs 4 assigned before damage may be assigned to B. CR 702.2b–c, 702.19b, 704.5h. |
| COM-11 | A's 2/2 first strike is blocked by B's plain 2/2. | First damage step: attacker deals 2, blocker deals none and dies at SBAs. A receives priority before the second damage step. The dead blocker never assigns normal damage; first striker does not deal again merely because the second step exists. CR 510.4, 702.7b, 117.5. |
| COM-12 | A's 2/2 double strike is blocked by B's plain 3/3. | First step marks 2 on blocker. Assuming both survive and no intervention, second step attacker deals 2 and blocker deals 3 simultaneously; both die at SBAs. Changing effects between the steps may change this result, so preserve the intervening priority window. CR 510.4, 702.4b. |
| COM-13 | A's 2/2 double strike without trample is blocked by B's 1/1, which dies in first damage. | No second-step damage reaches B; attacker remains blocked. With trample and a chosen first-step 1/1 split, B can take 1 in the first step and 2 in the second. Do not treat double strike as one lump of 4 damage. CR 509.1h, 510.4, 702.19d. |
| COM-14 | No combatant has first strike/double strike when the damage step begins. | There is one ordinary damage step, not an extra empty priority window representing first-strike damage. After ordinary damage/SBAs/triggers A receives priority. CR 510.3–4. |

Combat decisions must permit the legal assignments in COM-07/08, not only a heuristic's preferred answer. These examples do not cover all restrictions, requirements, banding, protection, battle/planeswalker attacks, multiple-block permissions or layer-dependent characteristics; unsupported processors remain explicit release blockers.

## Elimination, turn continuation and outcomes

| ID | Setup/action | Independent expected result and rule basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| END-01 | B reaches 0 life while an effect is resolving; that same effect later raises B above 0 before it finishes. | No life-loss SBA occurs in the middle of the resolution solely because B briefly reached 0. At the next check B survives if no loss condition remains. A stable 0-or-less total then causes loss before priority. CR 104.3b, 704.3–4. |
| END-02 | B's library is empty but B has not attempted a draw; then B must draw 1. | Empty library alone does not lose. The failed draw marks the loss condition, and B loses at the next SBA check. Trying to draw 3 with 1 remaining draws that 1 before the loss check. CR 104.3c, 704.5b. |
| END-03 | In 4-seat game B loses with A/C/D alive; B has priority. | Remove B and their owned objects; end effects giving B control; remove their noncard stack objects; exile any objects still controlled by B, in the rule's order. Priority passes to C. Do not award A the game merely for eliminating one opponent. CR 800.4a, 104.2a. |
| END-04 | B loses while A controls a B-owned permanent; B controls one A-owned permanent through a control effect. | B's owned permanent leaves regardless of A's control. End the effect giving B control of A's permanent; it normally returns to its previous/default eligible controller rather than being deleted just because B controlled it. More complex remaining-control cases follow 800.4a–c explicitly. |
| END-05 | Active player A leaves during their turn, and B/C/D remain. | Continue the current turn without an active player to its completion; do not instantly start B's turn. When A would receive priority, use 800.4j's next-player/resolution/step-end behavior. Skip turns that would belong to departed seats. CR 800.4j–k. |
| END-06 | Only A/B remain and B loses; separately, all remaining players lose simultaneously. | First state: A wins immediately because all opponents left. Second: draw, not an arbitrary lowest-index winner. No further ordinary gameplay decisions follow a terminal state. CR 104.2a, 104.4a. |
| END-07 | B leaves while an effect still needs B to choose/pay, or a B-controlled trigger awaits stacking. | A departed player does not get a new decision. Required payment is not paid; another choice is reassigned as 800.4g–h requires; a trigger that would be controlled by B is not put on stack. CR 800.4d, f–h. |

## Persistence and command expectations derived from the project contract

The following are application contract checks, not additional Magic rules. Apply them to selected states above instead of inventing different semantic expected results for each runtime:

- Save/reload during SET-03 bottom-card selection, PRI-03's response window, CMD-03's owner choice and COM-07's distribution decision. The pending semantic owner, legal constraints, object incarnations, stack, marked damage, historical commander counters and RNG state must resume consistently.
- Run the same accepted command/chance transcript from the same pinned source/IR/state in Bun and browser worker; compare canonical state, decision constraints and public projections. No filesystem/browser APIs belong in the rules core.
- Reject a stale revision, wrong actor, wrong decision identity, malformed payment, duplicate block assignment or illegal distribution without partial rules-state mutation. Retrying the exact accepted durable command returns its original receipt, rather than casting again or advancing a second turn.
- Inject persistence failure before commit: no accepted state or receipt becomes visible. After reload, only committed transitions exist. An unsupported or incompatible source/IR/schema save must fail explicitly rather than silently becoming a new game.
- Preserve private hand/library contents from nonowners in the public projections of every relevant scenario. Rules-authorized revelation is explicit; debugging a fixture is not permission to expose private data in normal views.

These scenarios are an initial independent expectation set. They do not cover the specification's full 12-family adversarial floor, certify any eligible card, or satisfy the required real-deck full-game runs. Each executable test should record the ID, pinned source hash, explicit setup, submitted actions, expected assertions, actual result and any unsupported classification.
